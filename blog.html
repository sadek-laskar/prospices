How to Check If Your Turmeric Is Pure

Bright yellow is not always a good sign. Here are two quick checks you can do at home.

Read the full article

Turmeric is one of the most commonly adulterated spices, because colour is easy to fake. Some cheap powders are bulked out with starch or tinted with artificial dyes to look brighter than they should.

Two simple home checks

  • Water test: Stir half a teaspoon into a glass of still water. Pure turmeric slowly sinks and gives a pale yellow tint. Powder that streaks bright yellow straight away may contain added colour.
  • Smell and taste: Real turmeric smells earthy and slightly bitter. A flat smell or a sharp chemical note is a warning sign.

These checks are a quick guide, not a laboratory test. For certainty, buy from brands that publish their sourcing and testing, and check for an FSSAI licence on the pack.

What to look for on the label

The ingredients list should say only "turmeric". Anything else, including "colour" or "permitted additives", is worth a second look.

Whole vs Ground Spices: Which Stays Fresh Longer?

Grinding exposes spices to air. Here is how that affects flavour and how to store each kind.

Read the full article

The flavour in a spice lives in its natural oils. When a spice is ground, far more of its surface meets air, so those oils fade faster. That is why whole spices generally stay potent much longer than ground ones.

A practical rule

  • Whole spices such as cumin, coriander seed and pepper can stay good for a couple of years if stored well.
  • Ground spices are best used within about a year, and sooner once the pack is open.

Storage tips

  • Use airtight containers, and keep them away from the stove and direct sunlight.
  • Always use a dry spoon. Moisture spoils spices quickly.
  • Buy smaller packs more often rather than one large pack that sits open for months.

If a spice has lost its smell when you rub a pinch between your fingers, it has lost most of its flavour too.

Garam Masala: What Goes In and When to Add It

Add it at the wrong time and the aroma disappears. Timing makes all the difference.

Read the full article

Garam masala is a blend of warming spices, and recipes vary from region to region and house to house. Common ingredients include cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, black pepper and cumin.

When to add it

Unlike turmeric or chilli, which cook in oil at the start, garam masala is a finishing spice. Its fragrant oils are delicate and fade with long cooking. Stir it in during the last few minutes, or sprinkle it over the dish just before serving.

How much to use

  • Start with half a teaspoon for a dish serving four, then taste and adjust.
  • Too much can make a dish bitter, so build up slowly.

Try it in dals, sabzis, rajma and biryani. Our Garam Masala is stone-ground in small batches so the aroma is strong even in small amounts.
